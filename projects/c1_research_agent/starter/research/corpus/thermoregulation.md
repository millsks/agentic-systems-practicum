# Keeping the Hive at the Right Temperature

Developing brood is sensitive to temperature, and honeybees keep the brood nest at roughly 34 to 35 degrees Celsius
whatever the weather outside. Bees raised at the wrong temperature can develop poorly.

To heat the nest, workers uncouple their wings and shiver their flight muscles, producing heat without flying. Some
bees press their warm bodies against capped brood cells or climb into empty cells between them to warm the brood from
the inside. To cool the nest, bees fan their wings in coordinated lines to move air, and foragers bring in water that
evaporates inside the hive. On hot days many bees cluster outside the entrance, a behavior beekeepers call bearding.

In winter there is no brood for part of the season, and the colony forms a tight cluster. Bees on the outside form an
insulating shell while those inside generate heat, fuelled by stored honey. The cluster moves slowly across the comb
to stay in contact with its food.
