# Propolis: Bee Glue

Propolis is a sticky material that bees make from resins collected from tree buds, sap flows, and other plant sources,
mixed with wax. Foragers carry the resin home on their hind legs, just as they carry pollen.

Bees use propolis to seal small cracks and gaps in the hive, to narrow the entrance, and to strengthen the comb. They
also coat the inner walls of the nest with a thin layer of it. Plant resins contain many compounds with antimicrobial
and antifungal properties, and research suggests that a propolis envelope helps the colony resist infections; some
studies describe it as part of the colony's social immunity.

If a mouse or other intruder dies inside a hive and is too large to remove, the bees may embalm it in propolis, which
prevents it from decaying and spreading disease. Beekeepers often find propolis gluing frames and lids together, and
it is collected and sold as a health product.
