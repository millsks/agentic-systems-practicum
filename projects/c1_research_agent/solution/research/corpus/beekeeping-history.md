# A Short History of Beekeeping

People collected honey from wild colonies long before they kept bees; rock paintings in Spain show honey hunting
thousands of years ago. Organized beekeeping appears in ancient Egypt, where reliefs from around 2400 BCE in the sun
temple of King Nyuserre show beekeepers working with hives and processing honey.

For centuries European beekeepers kept bees in straw baskets called skeps or in hollow logs. These hives had a serious
drawback: the comb was fixed to the walls, so harvesting honey usually meant destroying the comb and often the colony.

The modern hive dates from 1851, when the American clergyman Lorenzo Langstroth applied the idea of bee space: bees
leave a gap of roughly six to nine millimeters open as a passage, filling smaller gaps with propolis and larger ones
with comb. By hanging frames with exactly that gap between them and the hive walls, Langstroth created a hive whose
frames could be lifted out, inspected, and returned without harm. Movable-frame hives remain the standard today.
