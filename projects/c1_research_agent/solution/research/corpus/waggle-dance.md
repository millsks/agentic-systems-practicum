# The Waggle Dance

Foragers that find a rich food source tell their nestmates where it is with a dance performed on the vertical comb
inside the dark hive. The Austrian biologist Karl von Frisch decoded this language and shared the 1973 Nobel Prize in
Physiology or Medicine for the work.

In the waggle dance a bee runs a short straight line while shaking her abdomen from side to side, then loops back to
the start and repeats. The angle of the waggle run relative to straight up on the comb gives the direction of the
food relative to the sun: a run straight up means "fly toward the sun", and a run 40 degrees to the right of vertical
means "fly 40 degrees to the right of the sun". The duration of the waggle run encodes distance: the longer the run,
the farther the food.

For food very close to the hive, bees perform a simpler round dance that says "search nearby" without a precise
direction. Followers also learn the scent of the flowers from the dancer's body. Scouts use the same dance to
advertise possible nest sites during swarming.
