# Drones: The Male Bees

Drones are the male honeybees. They hatch from unfertilized eggs, so they carry only one set of chromosomes, inherited
entirely from their mother, the queen. Drones are larger and stouter than workers, have very large eyes for spotting
queens in flight, and have no stinger.

A drone has a single purpose: to mate with a virgin queen from another colony. On warm afternoons drones gather at
drone congregation areas, places in the air where drones from many colonies collect and wait for queens to pass
through. A drone that succeeds in mating dies immediately afterwards.

Drones do not forage, build comb, or defend the hive, and they depend on workers for food. A colony raises a few
hundred to a few thousand drones in spring and summer. When nectar runs short in autumn, workers stop feeding the
drones and drag them out of the hive, so a colony usually enters winter with no drones at all.
