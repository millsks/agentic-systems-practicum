# The Varroa Mite

Varroa destructor is a parasitic mite and the most serious pest of the western honeybee, Apis mellifera. It originally
lived on the Asian honeybee, Apis cerana, which has defenses against it. After western honeybees were brought into
contact with Asian bees in the twentieth century, the mite switched hosts and has since spread to almost every
beekeeping region in the world.

Female mites enter brood cells shortly before they are capped and reproduce on the developing bee. Mites feed mainly on
the fat body tissue of developing and adult bees, weakening them. Worse, varroa spreads viruses, especially deformed
wing virus, which can kill developing bees or leave adults with useless crumpled wings.

An untreated colony with a heavy mite load usually collapses within a few years. Beekeepers monitor mite levels and
use treatments such as organic acids, brood interruption, or breeding bees with hygienic behavior that detect and
remove infested brood. Varroa is considered a major driver of colony losses worldwide.
