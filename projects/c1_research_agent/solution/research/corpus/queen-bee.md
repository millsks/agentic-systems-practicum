# The Queen Bee

The queen is the only fully fertile female in a colony and the mother of almost every bee in it. Any fertilized egg
can become a queen. What decides the outcome is food: larvae destined to be queens are raised in large, peanut-shaped
cells and fed royal jelly throughout their development, while worker larvae are switched to a plainer diet after a
few days. A queen develops in about sixteen days, faster than a worker.

A few days after emerging, a young queen makes one or more mating flights and mates in the air with many drones,
often ten to twenty. She stores their sperm in an organ called the spermatheca and uses it for the rest of her life;
she never mates again. At the height of the season a good queen can lay well over a thousand eggs a day. She chooses
whether to fertilize each egg: fertilized eggs become females (workers or queens) and unfertilized eggs become drones.

Queens commonly live two to five years. The queen's pheromones tell the workers she is present and healthy; when that
signal fades, workers begin raising replacements.
