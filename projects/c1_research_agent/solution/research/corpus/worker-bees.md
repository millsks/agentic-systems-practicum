# Worker Bees and Their Jobs

Workers are females whose ovaries stay undeveloped while a healthy queen is present. They do nearly all the work of
the colony, and the job a worker does depends mostly on her age, a pattern called age polyethism.

In her first days a worker cleans and polishes cells. She then becomes a nurse, feeding larvae with secretions from
glands in her head. Around her second week her wax glands become active and she builds and repairs comb, processes
nectar into honey, and carries out dead bees. Toward the end of her third week she may guard the entrance, and after
that she becomes a forager, collecting nectar, pollen, water, and plant resins outside the hive.

The schedule is flexible: if the colony loses its foragers, younger bees speed up their development to replace them.
A summer worker lives only about five to six weeks, worn out by flying. Workers raised in autumn live for months,
because they spend the winter in a cluster keeping the queen warm instead of foraging.
