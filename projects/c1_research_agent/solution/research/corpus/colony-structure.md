# How a Honeybee Colony Is Organized

A honeybee colony is a single family that lives and works as one unit. In summer a strong colony holds one queen,
twenty to sixty thousand female worker bees, and a few hundred male drones. None of them can survive alone for long:
the queen cannot feed herself or build comb, workers cannot lay fertilized eggs, and drones cannot forage.

Biologists often call the colony a superorganism because the work that keeps it alive is divided among castes and
coordinated without any central command. The queen does not give orders. Decisions such as where to forage, when to
raise a new queen, or when to swarm emerge from thousands of small interactions between workers, passed along by
touch, dances, and chemical signals called pheromones.

The colony lives on wax comb: sheets of six-sided cells used both as a nursery for eggs and larvae (the brood) and as a
pantry for honey and pollen. See the documents on the queen, workers, drones, and beeswax for each part in detail.
